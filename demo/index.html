Metric Definitions
BLEU-4 : Corpus 4-gram precision vs references. Standard MT metric, lower for captioning due to lexical diversity.
METEOR : Unigram F-score with synonym matching via WordNet. More recall-sensitive than BLEU.
CIDEr : TF-IDF weighted n-gram consensus across all dataset references. Primary metric for image captioning benchmarks.
ROUGE-L : Longest common subsequence recall. Captures word order without requiring contiguous n-gram matches.
Distinct-2 : Ratio of unique bigrams to total bigrams across generated captions. Higher = more lexically diverse output. Nucleus sampling scores highest (~0.75-0.80).
Self-BLEU : BLEU of each caption against others in the same batch. Lower = captions are more distinct from each other. Best (lowest) for nucleus sampling. Highlighted cell = best (most diverse).

Analysis & Discussion
Findings • Trade-offs • Ethics
Key Findings
Finding 01
FT-4L achieves best CIDEr on COCO val (0.9364)
On COCO val2017 (500 images), FT-4L/beam tops all configs with CIDEr 0.9364 and BLEU-4 0.2917. LoRA performs lower (CIDEr 0.5556) on this dataset, suggesting full fine-tuning benefits from large-scale COCO training.
Finding 02
Beam search w=5 is optimal for COCO
CIDEr peaks at w=5 (0.6099) on the COCO sweep, not w=8 as seen on Flickr30k. This suggests optimal beam width is dataset-dependent. w=5 provides the best accuracy-latency tradeoff for real-time inference.
Finding 03
Temperature t=0.3 maximizes CIDEr for nucleus sampling
CIDEr drops monotonically from 0.4639 at t=0.3 to 0.0282 at t=1.2. Distinct-2 rises sharply above t=0.7, confirming the accuracy-diversity tradeoff. Conservative temperature (t=0.3) is recommended for factual captioning tasks.
Finding 04
Beam search consistently outperforms greedy and nucleus
Across all 3 models on COCO, beam search yields the highest CIDEr and BLEU-4. Greedy decoding matches beam for FT-4L (BLEU-4 0.288 vs 0.292) but fails for LoRA. Nucleus sampling adds diversity at 15-20% CIDEr cost.
Finding 05 — LoRA underperforms Frozen on COCO
Why LoRA scores lower than Frozen (CIDEr 0.556 vs 0.936)
This result is counter-intuitive but explainable by three compounding factors:

1. Rank too low for COCO scale. LoRA r=8 adds only ~2M trainable parameters on top of a 117M GPT-2. COCO has 590K captions with high lexical diversity. The adapter capacity is insufficient to absorb that variance, while Frozen + full ProjectionHead training directly optimizes the image-to-token bridge.

2. COCO evaluation rewards lexical precision. CIDEr uses TF-IDF weighting over all 5 COCO references per image. LoRA with nucleus sampling (CIDEr 0.362) generates more diverse but less reference-aligned captions, which is penalized by CIDEr more than by ROUGE-L.

Ethical Considerations
Dataset Bias
COCO captions reflect annotator biases. Western-centric scenes and gender stereotypes are overrepresented. Models trained on this data may underperform on diverse cultural contexts.
Misrepresentation Risk
Models generate confident but sometimes incorrect captions. Deployment in accessibility tools (alt-text generation) without human review could mislead visually impaired users. Human oversight is required for high-stakes applications.
Accessibility Value
Image captioning directly improves web accessibility for visually impaired users. COCO-trained models achieve CIDEr ~0.9 on common scenes, sufficient for assistive use cases when combined with human review workflows.
